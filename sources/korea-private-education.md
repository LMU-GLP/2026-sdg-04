# Spending on private education jumps 60% over past decade

- **Source:** Yonhap via *The Korea Herald*, January 4, 2026. Data from the Korean Statistical Information Service (KOSIS), Ministry of Data and Statistics.
- **URL:** https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10648305
- **Read on:** 2026-07-30

## Key figures

- Korean households spent just under **29.2 trillion won ($20.2 billion) on private education in 2024**, up **60.1% from 2014**. Fourth consecutive annual increase.
- By level, 2014 → 2024:
  - Elementary: 7.6 trillion → **13.2 trillion won (+74.1%)** — the biggest jump
  - Middle school: → 7.8 trillion won (+40.7%)
  - High school: → 8.1 trillion won (+60.5%)
- A family with at least two unmarried children spent an average of **611,000 won per month** on private education in Q3 2025.
- That is **12.6% of the family's entire monthly expenditure — second only to food.**
- Share of monthly household spending: 11.5% (2019) → 12.8% (2024) → 13.0% (Q1 2025) → **13.5% in Q2 2025, a record high.**

## Direct quotes

> "South Korean families' spending on private education for their children has jumped by more than 60 percent over the past decade"

> "Experts say frequent changes to college admission policies and resultant uncertainty are forcing parents to lean more heavily on private education."

## Why spending rises even as student numbers fall

The ministry gives two reasons: families have more money to spend on extra
lessons, and the number of dual-income households keeps growing — those parents
"often send their children to after-school hagwons during their working hours
rather than leaving them at home alone." So hagwons are partly functioning as
childcare.

## Note on figures you may see elsewhere

Claims that "78% of students attend hagwons," that Seoul students spend twice
what rural students do, or that tutoring costs 10% of household income and is
"the highest percentage worldwide" appear on commercial blogs, not in this
government data release. We could not verify them. Don't cite them without
finding a primary source.

## Summary

Korean families spent $20.2 billion on private education in 2024, and the share
of the household budget going to it hit a record 13.5% — more than anything
except food. Spending on elementary students grew fastest, partly because
after-school academies double as childcare for dual-income parents.
