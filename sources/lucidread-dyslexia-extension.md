# LucidRead — Free Browser Extension for Dyslexia Accommodations (California)

- **Source:** LucidRead product/policy page
- **URL:** https://www.lucidread.org/california.html
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- **"K–2 universal screening for reading difficulties including dyslexia risk"** began in California in the **2025–2026 school year**, under SB 114 (2023); AB 1369 (2015) had earlier required the state to develop dyslexia guidelines.
- California ranked **40th nationally** to implement universal dyslexia/reading-difficulty screening.
- Only **"29% of California 4th graders reading at or above Proficient."**
- An estimated **"20% of the general population"** experiences visual stress while reading.
- The site states California's law requires screening but has **no state-level mandate defining specific assistive-technology accommodations** — districts must name specific accommodations in each student's IEP without concrete guidance on what to use.

## What it is / how it works

LucidRead is a free Chrome browser extension (not a paid product or company-run program) built to give students with dyslexia in-browser reading accommodations: syllable splitting, text-to-speech, color overlays, a reading ruler, and morpheme highlighting — targeting the three difficulties the site names as core to dyslexia: decoding, visual stress, and tracking loss. It installs with no account, no configuration, and no data collection, and is described as "FERPA and COPPA compliant by design" because settings are stored only on-device.

## Summary

LucidRead is a free, no-signup Chrome extension built specifically to fill the gap between California's new legal requirement to screen students for dyslexia (as of 2025–2026) and its lack of any state mandate for what accommodations schools should actually provide — it is a concrete example of an existing tool already working on the accessibility side of this problem in the LA/California region.
