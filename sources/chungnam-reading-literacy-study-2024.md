# Chungnam Provincial Office of Education Literacy / Eye-Tracking Study (2024)

- **Source:** Study conducted by the Chungnam Provincial Office of Education (충청남도교육청) with 난독과 문해력 연구소 (the Dyslexia and Literacy Research Institute), using eye-tracking cameras on middle school (grades 1–3) and elementary 3rd-grade students. Reported in [아주대학보 (Ajou Press), 2026-04-07](https://press.ajou.ac.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=11374); corroborating figures and additional detail via a Chosun Ilbo report syndicated on [Daum, "중학생 92%가 1분에 지문 1개 못 읽는다"](https://v.daum.net/v/WQGkixIkZI?f=p).
- **URL:** https://press.ajou.ac.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=11374
- **Read on:** 2026-08-01

## Key figures

- Sample: **145 middle school students (grades 1–3)** and **97 elementary 3rd-grade students**.
- Method: researchers extracted 12–13 sentences (165–166 words) from grade-level Korean textbook passages and used eye-tracking cameras to observe whether each student finished reading within a time limit, and how their eyes moved across the text.
- "98% of elementary school students and 92% of middle school students showed backward eye movement and could not finish reading the textbook passage within the set time." — direct quote, Ajou Press.
- Corroborating detail (Daum/Chosun Ilbo): on a separate vocabulary-comprehension test on the same passages, **93% of elementary participants and 96% of middle school participants** scored below the benchmark (50–60 points); researchers described "zigzag-pattern eye movements" rather than normal left-to-right reading, which they attributed in part to short-form video habits.

## Summary

A 2024 Chungnam Provincial Office of Education study using eye-tracking on a small regional sample (145 middle schoolers, 97 elementary 3rd-graders) found 92% of middle school students and 98% of elementary students failed to finish reading a grade-level textbook passage within the allotted time. This is a general reading-fluency/literacy finding from a small regional sample — it says nothing about how many of these students have dyslexia specifically, and should not be cited as a dyslexia-prevalence figure.
