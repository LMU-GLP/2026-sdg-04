# L.A. Unified Sees 'Major Gains' in Fight Against Chronic Absenteeism – But Problem Persists

- **Source:** Jacob Matthews, *The 74*, June 13, 2025
- **URL:** https://www.the74million.org/article/l-a-unified-sees-major-gains-in-fight-against-chronic-absenteeism-but-problem-persists/
- **Read on:** 2026-07-30

## Key figures

- Chronic absenteeism is defined as **missing more than 10% of the school year.**
- LAUSD chronically absent students, by year:
  - 2021–22: **just over 45%** — the worst in district history
  - 2022–23: **36%**
  - 2023–24: **more than 32%** (latest finalized data)
  - 2024–25: not yet finalized; officials say gains continued but "could not quantify" them
- Nationally, chronic absenteeism **rose over 10% from 2019 to 2024**, peaking in 2022 at 28% of students (American Enterprise Institute).
- LAUSD is the nation's second-largest district.

## Direct quotes

> "Our approach is we support, we're not about penalizing." — Superintendent Alberto Carvalho

> "We have had some significant gains in chronic absenteeism, although we still have a lot of work to do. But we've seen some major gains, all across the board." — Rudy Gomez, director of iAttend, LAUSD's district-wide attendance program

## What the district actually does about it

Attendance counselors, principals, and Carvalho himself visit thousands of
families at home each year. They offer concrete fixes — free busing, new school
uniforms, "whatever could help." One mother told Carvalho that since the
pandemic she has been confused about when to keep a sick child home.

## Summary

Nearly half of LAUSD students were chronically absent in 2021–22, and the figure
has fallen each year since to just over 32%, still far above historic norms. The
district's own explanation for the improvement is unglamorous and specific:
knocking on doors, asking families what's in the way, and removing that thing.
