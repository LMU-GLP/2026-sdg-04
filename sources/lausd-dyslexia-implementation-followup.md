# LAist: How Can Schools Help Students With Dyslexia? In California, Responses Are Far From Consistent

- **URL:** https://laist.com/news/education/centralized-guidance-california-school-district-responses-to-dyslexia-literacy
- **Author:** Kyle Stokes
- **Published:** August 17, 2022
- **Date read:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- "More than 5,700 teachers — roughly one-quarter of LAUSD's teaching force — have received in-depth training on implementing a structured literacy approach to support struggling readers," per Maribel Luna, senior director for LAUSD's Division of Special Education.
- Another 850 paraprofessionals received the same training.
- In 2019, all district teachers and administrators received an overview of dyslexia research and district resources.

## Key quotes

- Sherry Rubalcava (retired LAUSD administrator), on accountability: "It needs to be something that the principal will insist upon. It's not enough for teachers to learn how to read..." — she said teachers often reverted to prior methods without principal follow-up.
- Luna's counter: LAUSD trains principals annually "on how to supervise structured literacy" and to discuss dyslexia's importance with parents.
- The article frames California's district-by-district dyslexia response, including LAUSD's, as inconsistent — training exists but is not uniformly mandatory or enforced.

## Summary

Five years after LAUSD's 2017 dyslexia resolution, roughly a quarter of the district's teachers (5,700+) and 850 paraprofessionals had received structured-literacy training, but a retired district administrator on the record says the training didn't come with the principal-level accountability needed to make it stick district-wide. This is a partial-implementation picture, not a confirmed full rollout or a confirmed failure — training happened at real scale, but enforcement was uneven by the district's own former staff's account.
