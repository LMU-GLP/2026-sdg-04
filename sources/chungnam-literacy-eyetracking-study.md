# Chungnam Provincial Office of Education Literacy / Eye-Tracking Study (2024)

- **Source:** 충청남도교육청 (Chungnam Provincial Office of Education), as reported by 아주대학보 (Ajou University student newspaper)
- **URL:** https://press.ajou.ac.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=11374
- **Read on:** 2026-08-01

## Key figures

- **"초등학생의 98%, 중학생의 92%가 정해진 시간 안에 교과서 지문을 읽지 못했다"** — 98% of elementary students and 92% of middle schoolers tested could not finish reading a grade-level textbook passage within the allotted time.
- In a separate self-diagnosis survey, **78% of surveyed middle schoolers said they "focus on short videos rather than reading"** ("독서보다 짧은 영상에 몰두한다").
- Methodology: eye-tracking cameras tracked students' gaze while they attempted to read excerpts (12–13 sentences / 165–166 words) drawn from grade-level Korean-language textbooks, within a time limit.

## What this figure is — and is not

This is a **general reading-fluency/literacy statistic**, not a dyslexia-prevalence statistic, and it comes from a regional study (Chungnam province), not a national one. The article does not report the exact sample size. It should be cited only to show that reading difficulty is visible and acute in Korean classrooms generally — not as evidence of how many students specifically have dyslexia or another diagnosable learning disability.

## Summary

A 2024 Chungnam Provincial Office of Education study using eye-tracking found that the large majority of both elementary (98%) and middle school (92%) students tested could not finish a grade-level textbook passage in the time given — a stark, if regional and small-sample, signal of a reading-fluency problem across the general student population, distinct from (though related to) the narrower question of how many students have a diagnosable reading disability like dyslexia.
