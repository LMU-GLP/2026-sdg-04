# 난독증 진단과 지도 가이드북 (입문용) — Dyslexia Diagnosis and Instruction Guidebook (Introductory Edition)

- **Source:** Incheon Metropolitan Office of Education Basic Academic Ability Support Center & Gyeongin National University of Education Basic Academic Ability Support Center. Published 2025.2.28. Lead author: Lee Dae-sik (이대식).
- **URL:** Not available — PDF provided directly to this repo; no public link found.
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- "전 세계적으로 전체 학생 중 적게는 5%에서 많게는 20% 정도의 학생이 난독증을 갖고 있는 것으로 추정되고 있을 정도로 흔한 학습장애의 한 유형입니다." — translated: **"Worldwide, an estimated 5% to 20% of all students have dyslexia** — common enough to be considered a typical type of learning disability."
- "우리나라에서도 1~5% 정도의 학생이 난독증을 갖고 있을 것으로 추정되고 있습니다." — translated: **"In Korea too, an estimated 1–5% of students are believed to have dyslexia."**
- Heritability: "가족 중 부모 중 어느 한 쪽이라도 난독증이 있을 경우 그 자녀가 난독증을 가질 확률은 40~60% 정도입니다. 형제 중 한 명이라도 난독증이 있다면 나머지 형제 중 난독증을 가질 확률은 그렇지 않은 경우에 비해 3~10배 정도 높은 것으로 알려져 있습니다." — translated: **"If either parent has dyslexia, a child's probability of having it is about 40–60%. If one sibling has dyslexia, the other siblings' probability is about 3–10 times higher than otherwise."**
- Legal definition (Incheon ordinance, effective 2024.7.15): "지능과 시력·청력이 모두 정상이고 듣고 말하는 데는 별다른 지장을 못 느낌에도 불구하고 언어와 관계되는 두뇌 신경회로에 문제가 있어 글을 원활하게 읽고 이해하는 데 어려움을 겪는 학습장애 증상" — translated: **"a learning-disability condition in which a person of normal intelligence, vision, and hearing, with no notable difficulty speaking or listening, nonetheless has trouble reading and understanding text fluently, due to a problem in the brain's language-related neural circuitry."**
- Recommended intervention intensity: **"주당 최소 3회 이상"** (at least 3 sessions per week), 10–15 minutes per session for phonological awareness instruction.
- Citing Shaywitz's 2015 Connecticut longitudinal study: reading-score gaps between strong and weak readers, once present, **persist essentially unchanged from about age 6 through age 16** — described in the guidebook as evidence dyslexia is "a chronic difficulty, not a temporary delay" ("일시적인 지체가 아닌 만성적인 어려움").

## What the program actually does

The guidebook packages a full school-level pipeline: screening tools (a 5-minute web-based test called 한글 또박또박, a 15-minute teacher-administered checklist), diagnostic instruments (KOLRA, BASA, NISE-B·ACT, CLT-R), an instructional method (phonics-based instruction over "whole-word" methods, explicitly citing research that whole-word instruction leaves students unable to decode new words), and a table of legally-grounded classroom accommodations: extra time on tests, text-to-speech/audiobooks, oral exam options, reduced homework load, and permission to use a keyboard in class.

## Direct quotes

> "읽기의 어려움을 보이는 학생에 대한 정확하고 빠른 진단은 가장 강력하고도 효과적인 개입입니다." — "Accurate, fast diagnosis of a student's reading difficulty is the single most powerful and effective intervention there is."

> "명시적이고 체계적인 글자-소리 대응 지도를 통해 손해 볼 것은 없지만, 그렇게 지도하지 않을 경우 손실은 너무나 크다." (quoting Kim, 2017) — "There is nothing to lose from explicit, systematic phonics instruction, but the cost of not providing it is enormous."

## Summary

This is a real, currently operating instructional framework from a South Korean regional education office: it combines screening, standardized diagnosis, phonics-based teaching methods, and legally mandated classroom accommodations for dyslexic students, estimating Korean dyslexia prevalence at 1–5% (versus a global estimate of 5–20%). It stresses that reading gaps between struggling and typical readers open early and stay open for a decade without intervention, making early screening and at least three weekly sessions of structured instruction central to its approach.
