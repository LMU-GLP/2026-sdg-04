## Key figures (translated from Korean; original figures quoted alongside)

- **"전 세계적으로 전체 학생 중 적게는 5%에서 많게는 20% 정도의 학생이 난독증을 갖고 있는 것으로 추정"** — "Worldwide, an estimated 5% to 20% of all students have dyslexia."
- **"우리나라에서도 1~5% 정도의 학생이 난독증을 갖고 있을 것으로 추정"** — "In our country [South Korea] as well, an estimated 1–5% of students are believed to have dyslexia."
- Heredity: **"가족 중 부모 중 어느 한 쪽이라도 난독증이 있을 경우 그 자녀가 난독증을 가질 확률은 40~60%"** — "If either parent has dyslexia, the probability their child has dyslexia is 40–60%." If a sibling has dyslexia, the risk to other siblings is **"3~10배 정도 높은 것"** — "about 3 to 10 times higher."
- Legal definition (Incheon City Dyslexia Student Support Ordinance, effective 2024.7.15): dyslexia is defined as a learning-disability condition where a student has normal intelligence, vision, and hearing, with no problem speaking or listening, "언어와 관계되는 두뇌 신경회로에 문제가 있어 글을 원활하게 읽고 이해하는 데 어려움을 겪는" — "a problem in the brain's language-related neural circuitry causing difficulty reading and understanding text smoothly."
- Recommended intensity of intervention: **"주당 최소 3회 이상"** — "at least 3 sessions per week, minimum."
- The guidebook explicitly rejects several popular myths, e.g.: **"시지각 훈련, 안구 운동, 컬러렌즈, 청음훈련 등은 난독증 치료를 위한 효과성이 증명되지 않았습니다"** — "Visual perception training, eye exercises, colored lenses, and auditory training have not been proven effective as dyslexia treatments."

## Direct quotes

> "난독증(dyslexia)이란 신경학적 원인으로 인해 나타나는 읽기 학습장애(learning disabilities)의 대표적인 하위 유형" — "Dyslexia is a representative subtype of reading-related learning disability arising from neurological causes."

> "읽기의 어려움을 보이는 학생에 대한 정확하고 빠른 진단은 가장 강력하고도 효과적인 개입입니다." — "Accurate, early diagnosis for students showing reading difficulty is the single most powerful and effective intervention."

## What the guidebook recommends

The guidebook, aimed at teachers and parents, lays out a full pipeline: school-level screening (1-minute oral reading fluency checks, a checklist tool, a free web-based Hangul test at ihangeul.kr), referral to standardized diagnostic tools (e.g., KOLRA, RA-RCP, NISE-B·ACT), and evidence-based instruction built on the five National Reading Panel (2000) pillars — phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension — plus specific school accommodations (extra time, text-to-speech/audiobooks, oral exams, reduced workload, keyboard use).

## Summary

An Incheon municipal education office and a national teacher-training university jointly published this 2025 guidebook because, despite dyslexia affecting an estimated 1–5% of Korean students, teachers and parents lack accessible guidance — it packages diagnosis, screening tools, and instructional methods (especially systematic phonics) into one practical reference for schools. It is direct evidence of a South Korean regional/local government actively building support infrastructure for this specific barrier.
