# Existing Programs and Products Already Working on This Problem

## LAUSD iAttend (Los Angeles, program)

A district-wide attendance program run by LAUSD. Attendance counselors, principals, and the superintendent visit thousands of families at home each year to identify and remove specific barriers (transportation, uniforms, confusion about sick-day policy). Framed explicitly as support rather than punishment: "Our approach is we support, we're not about penalizing." Chronic absenteeism has fallen from just over 45% (2021–22) to more than 32% (2023–24) since ramping up, though officials say a meaningful gap remains ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)).

## Incheon Metropolitan Office of Education dyslexia framework (South Korea, program)

A regional education office's full pipeline for dyslexia: free 5-minute web-based screening, standardized diagnostic tests (KOLRA, BASA, NISE-B·ACT, CLT-R), phonics-based instructional guidance for teachers, and a legally grounded table of classroom accommodations (extra test time, text-to-speech/audiobooks, oral exams, reduced homework, keyboard use). Recommends at least 3 structured reading-instruction sessions per week ([source](../sources/incheon-dyslexia-guidebook.md)).

## LucidRead (California, business/product)

A free Chrome browser extension providing dyslexia reading accommodations — text-to-speech, syllable/morpheme highlighting, color overlays, customizable typography, reading ruler/line-focus tools — built specifically because California's dyslexia screening law (SB 114, 2023) identifies at-risk readers but funds no specific technology response. Runs on Chromebooks districts already own, at no cost, with no account or tracking required ([source](../sources/lucidread-dyslexia-accommodations.md)).

> "LucidRead eliminates the technology accommodation cost entirely: it is free, requires no accounts, and runs on the Chromebooks districts already own." ([source](../sources/lucidread-dyslexia-accommodations.md))

## What this tells us

All three existing efforts target barriers *after* a student is already identified as at-risk (chronically absent, or screened for dyslexia). None of the sources here describe a program that improves *identification itself* in Korea — which is precisely the gap Shin & Choi describe (see [Dyslexia and Learning Disabilities](dyslexia-learning-disabilities.md)). That's a candidate gap for a team solution, not a claim any source makes directly.
