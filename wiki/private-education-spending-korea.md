# Private Education Spending in South Korea

If access to extra academic help depends on family income, that's a barrier baked into the
system rather than the school building — and Korea's own data shows this barrier is
growing, not shrinking.

## The numbers

Korean households spent just under **29.2 trillion won ($20.2 billion) on private education
in 2024, up 60.1% from 2014** — the fourth consecutive annual increase.
([Korea private education spending](../sources/korea-private-education.md))

By school level, 2014 → 2024 growth:

| Level | 2024 spending | Growth since 2014 |
|---|---|---|
| Elementary | 13.2 trillion won | **+74.1%** (largest jump) |
| Middle school | 7.8 trillion won | +40.7% |
| High school | 8.1 trillion won | +60.5% |

([Korea private education spending](../sources/korea-private-education.md))

A family with at least two unmarried children spent an average of **611,000 won per month**
on private education in Q3 2025 — **12.6% of the family's entire monthly expenditure,
second only to food.** The share of monthly household spending going to private education
has climbed every year measured: **11.5% (2019) → 12.8% (2024) → 13.0% (Q1 2025) → 13.5%
(Q2 2025, a record high).** ([Korea private education spending](../sources/korea-private-education.md))

## Why it's rising even as the student population shrinks

The ministry cites two reasons: rising household income, and growing numbers of dual-income
households who **"often send their children to after-school hagwons during their working
hours rather than leaving them at home alone"** — meaning hagwons are partly functioning as
childcare, not just supplemental instruction.
([Korea private education spending](../sources/korea-private-education.md))

The source also points to policy churn as a driver: "frequent changes to college admission
policies and resultant uncertainty are forcing parents to lean more heavily on private
education." That means the cost barrier isn't purely a preference — it compounds for
families who can't afford it, for both academic and childcare reasons.
([Korea private education spending](../sources/korea-private-education.md))

## Related barrier: under-identification of learning disabilities

A separate and distinct barrier in Korea — not a cost barrier — is that officially
identified learning-disability rates have fallen even as reading-difficulty surveys suggest
a stable at-risk population. See
[Dyslexia and Learning Disabilities in Korea](dyslexia-learning-disabilities.md).

## A caution about numbers not in this dataset

Figures circulating elsewhere — "78% of students attend hagwons," Seoul students spending
double what rural students do, tutoring costing "10% of household income, the highest
percentage worldwide" — do **not** appear in this government release and could not be
verified. Per the source itself: "Don't cite them without finding a primary source."
([Korea private education spending](../sources/korea-private-education.md))
