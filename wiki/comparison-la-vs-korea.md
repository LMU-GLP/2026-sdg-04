# Los Angeles vs. South Korea: Comparing the Evidence

The region for this project is still open. This page lays out what our sources actually
say about each, side by side — without forcing a comparison the data doesn't support.

## What each region's evidence covers

| | Los Angeles | South Korea |
|---|---|---|
| Core barrier documented | Chronic absenteeism (missing >10% of school year) | (1) Cost-driven private tutoring dependence; (2) under-identification of learning disabilities |
| Headline figure | **32%+** of LAUSD students chronically absent, 2023–24 ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)) | **13.5%** of household spending goes to private education, Q2 2025 ([source](../sources/korea-private-education.md)); **0.02%** of school-age population identified with LD vs. an estimated **1–5%** at risk ([source](../sources/korea-learning-disabilities-invisible-population.md)) |
| Direction of trend | Improving: down from 45% (2021–22) to 32%+ (2023–24) ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)) | Both worsening: tutoring spend share up every year since 2019 ([source](../sources/korea-private-education.md)); LD identification rate down from 8.7% (2009) to 1.5% (2019) ([source](../sources/korea-learning-disabilities-invisible-population.md)) |
| What the district/system says is the fix | Home visits, removing specific logistical barriers (busing, uniforms) ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)) | Clearer legal definitions, unified identification criteria, more special-education staffing, public-awareness campaigns ([source](../sources/korea-learning-disabilities-invisible-population.md)) |

## Why we are **not** putting these numbers in one chart

Per this wiki's citation rules, we do not compare a figure from one country to a figure from
another unless both measure the same thing the same way. Chronic absenteeism (a measure of
physical presence) and LD identification rate or private-tutoring spending share (measures
of resourcing and diagnosis) are not the same construct, and none of our three headline
figures — 32%, 13.5%, 0.02% — are directly comparable to one another. Any of these numbers
used side-by-side in a pitch should be presented as three separate facts, not as a single
ranking of "which region has it worse."

## What is comparable in spirit, if not in number

Both regions show a barrier that is **structural rather than individual** — a student's
control over the outcome is limited:

- In LA, a student can't fix a family's transportation problem or a parent's confusion about
  school policy on their own.
- In Korea, a student with an undiagnosed learning disability can't fix a broken
  identification system, and a family's ability to pay for tutoring is itself set by income.

## Which region has more evidence behind it right now

South Korea has the deeper paper trail for a *disability-specific* framing (an academic
paper with decade-long government statistics, plus a real product working the same problem
in the LA/California source material — see [Reading-Support Technology](reading-support-technology.md)).
Los Angeles has the stronger paper trail for a *systemic/logistical* framing (a recent,
detailed news account of program mechanics and multi-year district data). Neither region
currently has a source connecting the two lenses (disability + logistics) in the same
place — that is a real gap in this wiki, not a decided answer about which region to pick.
