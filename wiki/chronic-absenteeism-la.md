# Chronic Absenteeism in Los Angeles

A student who simply isn't in the building can't be reached by any classroom intervention —
this is one of the most basic "barriers at school" there is.

## The numbers

Chronic absenteeism is defined as **missing more than 10% of the school year.**
([LA chronic absenteeism](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md))

LAUSD (the nation's second-largest school district) chronically-absent rate, by year:

| Year | Chronically absent |
|---|---|
| 2021–22 | **just over 45%** — worst in district history |
| 2022–23 | **36%** |
| 2023–24 | **more than 32%** (latest finalized figure) |
| 2024–25 | not yet finalized; officials say gains continued but "could not quantify" them |

([LA chronic absenteeism](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md))

For context, chronic absenteeism **nationally rose over 10% from 2019 to 2024, peaking in
2022 at 28% of students** (American Enterprise Institute, cited in the same source). LAUSD's
worst year (45%) was well above even that national peak.

## What's actually driving the improvement

LAUSD's own explanation is specific and low-tech: attendance counselors, principals, and
the superintendent visit thousands of families at home each year, offering concrete fixes —
free busing, new uniforms, "whatever could help."
([LA chronic absenteeism](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)) One parent told the
superintendent she'd been confused since the pandemic about when to keep a sick child home —
suggesting some of the barrier is informational, not structural.

> "Our approach is we support, we're not about penalizing." — Superintendent Alberto Carvalho
> ([LA chronic absenteeism](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md))

## Open question

The source is explicit that 2024–25 numbers aren't finalized — officials claim continued
progress but "could not quantify" it. Any pitch that cites an LA absenteeism figure should
use the 32% (2023–24) figure, not a guess at the current year.
