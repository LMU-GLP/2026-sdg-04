# South Korea: the cost of private education (hagwons)

Korean households spent just under **29.2 trillion won ($20.2 billion)** on
private education in 2024, up **60.1%** from 2014 — the fourth consecutive
annual increase
[[Korea Herald/KOSIS]](../sources/korea-private-education.md).

## Where the growth is concentrated

By level, 2014 → 2024:

- Elementary: 7.6 trillion → **13.2 trillion won (+74.1%)** — the biggest jump
- Middle school: → 7.8 trillion won (**+40.7%**)
- High school: → 8.1 trillion won (**+60.5%**)

[[Korea Herald/KOSIS]](../sources/korea-private-education.md)

## The household budget squeeze

A family with at least two unmarried children spent an average of **611,000 won
per month** on private education in Q3 2025 — **12.6%** of the family's entire
monthly expenditure, second only to food
[[Korea Herald/KOSIS]](../sources/korea-private-education.md). The share of
monthly household spending going to private education has climbed every period
measured: **11.5%** (2019) → **12.8%** (2024) → **13.0%** (Q1 2025) → **13.5%**
in Q2 2025, a record high
[[Korea Herald/KOSIS]](../sources/korea-private-education.md).

## Why spending rises even as enrollment falls

The ministry gives two reasons, per the source: families have more money for
extra lessons, and dual-income households increasingly use hagwons as
after-school childcare rather than leaving children unsupervised
[[Korea Herald/KOSIS]](../sources/korea-private-education.md).

> "Experts say frequent changes to college admission policies and resultant uncertainty are forcing parents to lean more heavily on private education." — [[Korea Herald/KOSIS]](../sources/korea-private-education.md)

## A caution on numbers not in this source

Figures sometimes cited elsewhere — "78% of students attend hagwons," Seoul
students spending twice what rural students do, or tutoring costing "10% of
household income, the highest percentage worldwide" — do **not** appear in this
government data release and were not verified. Don't repeat them without a
primary source
[[Korea Herald/KOSIS]](../sources/korea-private-education.md).

## Why this is a different kind of barrier than LA's

This is a cost and pressure barrier layered *on top of* full school
attendance — not students missing school, but families spending a record share
of their budget on supplemental instruction outside it. See
[LA vs. South Korea](la-vs-south-korea.md).
