# Los Angeles vs. South Korea: Comparing the Evidence

Per CLAUDE.md's rules, figures from one region are not compared to figures from another unless both measure the same thing the same way. **They mostly don't, here** — so this page lays out what each region's evidence actually measures, side by side, without averaging or equating them.

| | Los Angeles | South Korea |
|---|---|---|
| **Documented barrier type** | Attendance (physically missing school) | Cost (private tutoring spend) *and* under-identification (learning disabilities/dyslexia) |
| **Headline figure** | More than 32% of LAUSD students chronically absent, 2023–24 ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)) | 13.5% of household monthly spending went to private education, Q2 2025, a record high ([source](../sources/korea-private-education.md)) |
| **Trend direction** | Improving: 45%→36%→32%+ since 2021–22 ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)) | Worsening: spending share up from 11.5% (2019) to 13.5% (Q2 2025) ([source](../sources/korea-private-education.md)) |
| **Existing program response** | LAUSD iAttend — home visits, concrete fixes ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)) | Incheon dyslexia screening/instruction framework ([source](../sources/incheon-dyslexia-guidebook.md)) |
| **Reading-specific data** | California: only 29% of 4th graders reading proficient (2024 NAEP) ([source](../sources/lucidread-dyslexia-accommodations.md)) | Officially identified LD rate: 1.5% of special-ed students (2019), down from 8.7% (2009); separate surveys suggest 1–5% of *all* students struggle with reading ([source](../sources/korea-learning-disabilities-prevalence.md)) |

## Why these numbers can't be averaged or directly compared

- **Chronic absenteeism (LA) and private-education spending share (Korea) measure completely different things** — one is a rate of missed school days, the other is a share of household budget. Neither source offers the other region's equivalent metric.
- **California's 29% reading-proficiency figure and Korea's 1.5%/1–5% learning-disability figures are not the same measurement either.** NAEP proficiency measures reading skill across an entire student population against grade-level standards; Korea's figures measure formal special-education *identification* of a specific disability category. A student can be a "non-proficient reader" without being identified as LD, and vice versa.
- No source here reports chronic absenteeism data for South Korea, or private-tutoring / learning-disability-identification data for Los Angeles. We cannot say which region's problem is "worse" — only that the two regions' available evidence documents different kinds of barriers.

## What each region's evidence is strongest on

- **LA** has the clearest trend data and a named, evaluated program (iAttend) with multi-year numbers.
- **Korea** has the richer evidence on *why* a barrier persists — cost pressure compounding with policy uncertainty, and a documented identification/diagnosis gap for learning disabilities — but weaker district-level trend data comparable to LAUSD's.

## What is comparable in spirit, if not in number

Both regions show a barrier that is **structural rather than individual** — a student's
control over the outcome is limited:

- In LA, a student can't fix a family's transportation problem or a parent's confusion about
  school policy on their own.
- In Korea, a student with an undiagnosed learning disability can't fix a broken
  identification system, and a family's ability to pay for tutoring is itself set by income.

## Which region has more evidence behind it right now

South Korea has the deeper paper trail for a *disability-specific* framing (an academic
paper with decade-long government statistics, plus a real product working the same problem
in the LA/California source material — see [Reading-Support Technology](reading-support-technology.md)).
Los Angeles has the stronger paper trail for a *systemic/logistical* framing (a recent,
detailed news account of program mechanics and multi-year district data). Neither region
currently has a source connecting the two lenses (disability + logistics) in the same
place — that is a real gap in this wiki, not a decided answer about which region to pick.

This is a decision point for the team, not a conclusion any source reaches for us: pick the region based on which specific, well-evidenced barrier you can defend out loud, not on which country "sounds" worse.
