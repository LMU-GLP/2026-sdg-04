# Problem Statement

## Region

**Los Angeles**, specifically the **Los Angeles Unified School District (LAUSD)** — the second-largest school district in the United States ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)).

## Who is affected, and how many

Two distinct populations, measured two different ways — they should not be added together or treated as the same count:

- In June 2017, the **LAUSD Board of Education** passed a resolution stating that "up to **20% of the overall population** displays some signs or symptoms of dyslexia," which it translates to roughly **128,000 at-risk students within LAUSD** ([source](../sources/lausd-dyslexia-resolution.md)). This is a "signs or symptoms" estimate from the district itself, **not** a confirmed clinical diagnosis count.
- On the 2024 NAEP, "**only 29% of California 4th graders reading at proficient level**" ([source](../sources/lucidread-dyslexia-accommodations.md)) — meaning roughly 71% are not. This is a general statewide reading-proficiency measure, not a dyslexia-specific count, and it is not LAUSD-specific.

## What our sources actually say about them

- The 2017 LAUSD resolution committed the district to: reviewing all dyslexia-related policy within 90 days, training teachers and staff on dyslexia warning signs and evidence-based **Structured Literacy** instruction, providing accommodations and modifications, and ensuring multisensory, explicit, structured reading instruction ([source](../sources/lausd-dyslexia-resolution.md)).
- Five years on, a 2022 report found LAUSD had trained "more than 5,700 teachers — roughly one-quarter of LAUSD's teaching force" plus 850 paraprofessionals in structured literacy, and gave all teachers and administrators a 2019 dyslexia-research overview ([source](../sources/lausd-dyslexia-implementation-followup.md)). That is partial, real-scale follow-through — **not** a full rollout across the district's teaching force, and not nothing either.
- The same report quotes a retired LAUSD administrator, Sherry Rubalcava, saying the training lacked principal-level accountability: "It needs to be something that the principal will insist upon. It's not enough for teachers to learn how to read..." — she says teachers often reverted to prior methods without that follow-up ([source](../sources/lausd-dyslexia-implementation-followup.md)). LAUSD's Maribel Luna countered that principals get annual training on supervising structured literacy. Both statements are on the record; our sources don't let us adjudicate between them.
- At the state level, California's **SB 114 (2023)** mandates universal K–2 screening for dyslexia risk, but the law funds **no specific technology accommodation** for students once they're screened. A free tool, LucidRead, exists specifically to fill that unfunded gap for districts ([source](../sources/lucidread-dyslexia-accommodations.md)).
- Separately, LAUSD has a well-documented, different barrier — chronic absenteeism (more than 32% of students in 2023–24, down from just over 45% in 2021–22) — but **no source here draws any connection** between absenteeism and dyslexia or reading proficiency; it is a distinct issue ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)).

## The problem statement

Within LAUSD, the district's own 2017 resolution estimates roughly 128,000 students show signs of dyslexia, and statewide, only 29% of California 4th graders currently read at a proficient level. LAUSD did follow through on part of its 2017 resolution — training over 5,700 teachers (about a quarter of its teaching force) and 850 paraprofessionals in structured literacy by 2022 — but that leaves roughly three-quarters of teachers untrained, and a retired district administrator on the record says the training that did happen lacked the principal-level accountability to make it stick. Meanwhile California's 2023 screening law identifies at-risk readers statewide without funding any specific technology to help teachers act on that identification. The gap we can defend is not "LAUSD did nothing" — real, at-scale training happened — it's that **training and screening reached only part of the district and came without enforcement, while the state funds identification but not the classroom tool to act on it**, leaving a real but unquantified number of LAUSD students flagged or at-risk without a trained teacher and a funded accommodation tool both in place.

## What we still can't defend, and shouldn't claim in a pitch

- Whether the "roughly one-quarter trained" figure is still accurate today — it's from a 2022 report, and we have no more recent source on LAUSD's training coverage.
- Which side of the Rubalcava/Luna disagreement over principal accountability is correct — both are on the record, and we can't adjudicate between them from these sources.
- Any causal link between LAUSD's chronic absenteeism rate and dyslexia or reading proficiency — no source connects these.
- An LAUSD-specific (rather than statewide) reading-proficiency number — our 29%/71% figure is California-wide, not LAUSD-only.
- Treating "128,000 at-risk" and "71% not proficient" as the same population, or addable — they are two different measurements from two different sources.
