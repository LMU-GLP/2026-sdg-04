# Los Angeles vs. South Korea: two different barriers, not two versions of one number

We haven't picked a region yet. Here's what each candidate source actually
measures — and a caution before comparing them.

## What each source measures

| | Los Angeles (LAUSD) | South Korea |
|---|---|---|
| What's measured | Share of enrolled students **absent** more than 10% of the school year [[The 74]](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md) | Household **spending** on private supplemental education [[Korea Herald/KOSIS]](../sources/korea-private-education.md) |
| Headline figure | **32%+** chronically absent in 2023–24, down from **45%+** in 2021–22 [[The 74]](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md) | **29.2 trillion won ($20.2B)** spent in 2024, up **60.1%** in a decade [[Korea Herald/KOSIS]](../sources/korea-private-education.md) |
| Direction of trend | Falling since 2021–22 [[The 74]](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md) | Rising every year measured [[Korea Herald/KOSIS]](../sources/korea-private-education.md) |
| Kind of barrier | Students not present in the classroom at all | Students present in school, but families paying a record share of income for education outside it |
| Who's named as responding | The district itself (attendance counselors, home visits) [[The 74]](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md) | Not a program response in our source — the source explains the cause (admissions-policy uncertainty, dual-income childcare need), not a fix [[Korea Herald/KOSIS]](../sources/korea-private-education.md) |

## Why we are not putting these two figures side by side as "bigger vs. smaller"

Per our own citation rules: don't compare a figure from one country to a figure
from another unless both measure the same thing the same way. A percentage of
students absent and a dollar amount of household spending are not the same
measurement, so there's no meaningful "LA's number is worse than Korea's number"
statement to make here. What can be said: LA's source is about **access** (is
the student physically at school), and Korea's is about **cost/pressure**
(what does a family have to pay and endure once the student is enrolled). Both
are barriers to learning; they are different barriers.

## What's missing to make this a fair comparison

Neither source gives us the other region's version of its own metric — we don't
have LAUSD private-tutoring spending, and we don't have a South Korean chronic
absenteeism rate. Until one of those exists, this page can only describe each
region's documented barrier on its own terms, not rank them.

## Where each connects to SDG 4

LA's absenteeism problem sits closest to Target 4.1 ("complete... primary and
secondary education") and Target 4.a (safe, supportive learning environments).
South Korea's spending problem sits closest to Target 4.5's concern with
equal access "for the vulnerable" if lower-income families can't keep pace with
rising hagwon costs — though our source doesn't break spending down by income
bracket, so that link is inference, not a cited figure. See
[SDG 4 targets](sdg4-targets.md).
