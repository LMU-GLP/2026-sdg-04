# Los Angeles: Chronic Absenteeism as a Barrier to Learning

Chronic absenteeism — missing more than 10% of the school year — is a barrier that
keeps students out of the classroom entirely, before any question of engagement or
curriculum even applies
([L.A. Unified chronic absenteeism](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)).

## The scale of the problem

LAUSD, the nation's second-largest school district, saw chronic absenteeism climb to
**"just over 45%" of students in 2021–22** — "the worst in district history" — before
falling in each subsequent year: 36% in 2022–23, and "more than 32%" in 2023–24 (the
latest finalized figure). District officials say gains continued into 2024–25 but
"could not quantify" them yet
([L.A. Unified chronic absenteeism](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)).

Nationally, chronic absenteeism "rose over 10% from 2019 to 2024, peaking in 2022 at
28% of students," per the American Enterprise Institute
([L.A. Unified chronic absenteeism](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)) — so even
LAUSD's improved 32% rate remains well above the national peak.

## What LAUSD says is actually working

The district's own explanation for the improvement is not a new curriculum or
technology — it's basic outreach: attendance counselors, principals, and even
Superintendent Alberto Carvalho visiting thousands of families at home each year,
offering concrete fixes like free busing and school uniforms. As Carvalho put it,
"Our approach is we support, we're not about penalizing"
([L.A. Unified chronic absenteeism](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)). One parent
told him she'd been confused, since the pandemic, about when it's appropriate to keep
a sick child home — a small, specific confusion that home visits catch and a
district-wide policy might miss.

## Open question

The article doesn't break out *why* students are chronically absent by cause (illness,
transportation, disengagement, mental health, etc.), so we can't yet say from this
source alone whether reading difficulties or other learning disabilities are a
meaningful driver of LA absenteeism specifically — see
[Dyslexia and Learning Disabilities](dyslexia-learning-disabilities.md) for what we do
know about that barrier, documented mainly in the Korean sources.
