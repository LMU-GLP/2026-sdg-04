# Los Angeles: chronic absenteeism in LAUSD

LAUSD, the nation's second-largest school district, defines chronic absenteeism
as missing more than 10% of the school year
[[The 74]](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md).

## The trend

- 2021–22: **just over 45%** of LAUSD students chronically absent — the worst in
  district history
- 2022–23: **36%**
- 2023–24: **more than 32%** (latest finalized data)
- 2024–25: not yet finalized; officials say gains continued but "could not
  quantify" them

[[The 74]](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)

For context, chronic absenteeism rose nationally by **over 10%** from 2019 to
2024, peaking in 2022 at **28%** of students, per the American Enterprise
Institute figure cited in the same article
[[The 74]](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md). LAUSD's 2021–22 rate (just over
45%) was well above that national peak, though the two figures come from
different sources measuring different populations, so treat them as context,
not a like-for-like comparison.

## What the district says is working

Superintendent Alberto Carvalho and attendance counselors visit thousands of
families at home each year, offering concrete fixes: free busing, new school
uniforms, "whatever could help"
[[The 74]](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md). The district's own framing:

> "Our approach is we support, we're not about penalizing." — Superintendent Alberto Carvalho, [[The 74]](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)

> "We have had some significant gains in chronic absenteeism, although we still have a lot of work to do." — Rudy Gomez, director of iAttend, [[The 74]](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)

## Why this is a "barriers to school" story, not an engagement story

This source describes students missing school entirely — a physical-access and
support barrier (transportation, uniforms, family confusion about attendance
rules), not a question of whether the curriculum is engaging once they're
there. Worth keeping distinct from South Korea's problem; see
[LA vs. South Korea](la-vs-south-korea.md).
