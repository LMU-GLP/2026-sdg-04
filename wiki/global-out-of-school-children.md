# The scale of the problem globally

UNESCO's 2024 Global Education Monitoring Report puts the number of children and
youth out of school worldwide at **251 million**
[[UNESCO GEM Report 2024]](../sources/unesco-out-of-school-children-2024.md).
That figure has fallen by **only 1%** in the nearly ten years since the SDGs were
adopted in 2015, even though **110 million** more children and youth have entered
school and **40 million** more have finished secondary school over that period
[[UNESCO GEM Report 2024]](../sources/unesco-out-of-school-children-2024.md).

## Access is not distributed evenly

- **33%** of school-aged children and youth are out of school in low-income
  countries, versus **3%** in high-income countries
  [[UNESCO GEM Report 2024]](../sources/unesco-out-of-school-children-2024.md).
- Spending per learner: **$8,543** in high-income countries versus **$55** in
  low- and middle-income countries
  [[UNESCO GEM Report 2024]](../sources/unesco-out-of-school-children-2024.md).
- **Sub-Saharan Africa** hosts more than half of all out-of-school children and
  adolescents globally
  [[UNESCO GEM Report 2024]](../sources/unesco-out-of-school-children-2024.md).
- Global aid to education fell from **9.3%** of total aid (2019) to **7.6%**
  (2022) [[UNESCO GEM Report 2024]](../sources/unesco-out-of-school-children-2024.md).

## Why this page matters for LA and South Korea

Neither Los Angeles nor South Korea is a low-income country, so neither shows up
in the "out of school" numbers above — the barriers in our two candidate regions
are different in kind from the global out-of-school crisis, not a smaller version
of it. LA's barrier, per our source, is chronic absenteeism among *enrolled*
students [[The 74]](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md). South Korea's, per our
source, is cost and pressure from private supplemental education, not lack of
access to school itself
[[Korea Herald]](../sources/korea-private-education.md). See
[LA vs. South Korea](la-vs-south-korea.md) for that distinction in full — the
global figures on this page are not directly comparable to either region's
numbers, since they measure a different thing (enrollment/access) than
attendance or spending do.
