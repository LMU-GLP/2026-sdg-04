# SDG 4: Quality Education — The Global Target Our Challenge Sits Inside

Our challenge — "How might we make learning more accessible, engaging, or useful for
students who face barriers at school?" — maps most directly onto **UN Sustainable
Development Goal 4** and specifically **Target 4.5**, which calls to "eliminate gender
disparities in education and ensure equal access to all levels of education and
vocational training for the vulnerable, including persons with disabilities" by 2030
([UN SDG 4 targets](../sources/sdg4-un-targets.md)).

## What the global indicators show

- Global completion rates in 2024 were "88% in primary, 78% in lower secondary, and
  61% in upper secondary education" ([UN SDG 4 targets](../sources/sdg4-un-targets.md)) —
  the barrier compounds as students get older.
- Target 4.5's own indicator tracks parity by "disability status" alongside gender,
  wealth, and geography. The wealth-based version of this gap "is 0.91 in primary,
  0.68 in lower secondary, and 0.34 in upper secondary education"
  ([UN SDG 4 targets](../sources/sdg4-un-targets.md)) — meaning the poorest students fall
  further behind the richest at every stage. No global disability-parity figure was
  reported on the UN page itself, only that it is one of the tracked categories.
- Target 4.a on inclusive facilities notes "over a fifth of primary schools globally do
  not have access to basic services, such as electricity, drinking water and basic
  sanitation facilities" ([UN SDG 4 targets](../sources/sdg4-un-targets.md)).

## Why this matters for our region choice

These are worldwide aggregates — they don't tell us anything specific about Los
Angeles or South Korea, and Target 4.5's disability-parity indicator has no reported
figure at all on the source page. We're using this page as the framework the rest of
the wiki responds to, not as data to compare against our regional numbers (see
[Los Angeles vs. South Korea](la-vs-south-korea.md) for why we don't average figures
across countries).
