# Los Angeles: Chronic Absenteeism as a Barrier to Learning

## The core problem

LAUSD — the second-largest school district in the U.S. — defines chronic absenteeism as **missing more than 10% of the school year**. By that measure, chronically absent students fell from **just over 45%** in 2021–22 (the district's worst year on record) to **36%** in 2022–23 to **more than 32%** in 2023–24, the latest finalized year. District officials say 2024–25 kept improving but "could not quantify" it yet ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)).

Nationally, chronic absenteeism **rose over 10% from 2019 to 2024**, peaking in 2022 at 28% of students, according to the American Enterprise Institute figures cited in the same article ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)).

## What the district is already doing

LAUSD runs iAttend, a district-wide attendance program. Attendance counselors, principals, and Superintendent Alberto Carvalho himself visit thousands of families at home each year, offering concrete fixes — free busing, new uniforms, "whatever could help." One parent told Carvalho she'd been confused, since the pandemic, about when it's appropriate to keep a sick child home ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md)).

> "Our approach is we support, we're not about penalizing." — Superintendent Alberto Carvalho ([source](../sources/la-chronic-absenteeism.md))

## Related: reading proficiency in California

Separately, California's 2024 NAEP results show only **29% of California 4th graders reading at proficient level** — meaning roughly 71% are not — and California was only the 40th state to mandate universal dyslexia screening ([source](../sources/lucidread-dyslexia-accommodations.md)). This is a different metric from chronic absenteeism (it measures reading skill, not attendance), and no source here connects the two directly for LA specifically.

## What's not established here

No source in this wiki explains *why* LAUSD students are absent (beyond the one anecdote about uncertainty over sick-day policy), nor does any source give LA-specific data on learning disabilities or dyslexia identification rates.
